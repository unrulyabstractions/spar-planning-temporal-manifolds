Planning Temporal Manifolds · literature brief · main direction

How to show that horizon steering changes real advice

The team's main direction is to steer a model towards myopic or long-term behaviour in realistic tasks, measured above baseline noise. This brief collects what the literature says about how to build those realistic scenarios, what to measure (and what not to), which baselines a reviewer will expect, and how to run the "are you sure?" flip test.

00 · Summary

Real requests, one option ladder per scenario, two baselines

Build

  • Scenarios seeded from real chatbot requests (WildChat), rewritten as templates. Career first; finance and health as replications.
  • Each scenario gets a ladder of ~8 concrete actions ordered by when they pay off, asked in every answer format (2-choice to free-form).
  • Horizon and risk vary separately across the ladder.

Measure

  • The horizon of the action the model recommends, in log time, from probabilities where possible.
  • For free-form: a validated judge maps the advice back onto the ladder. Headline: "advice moved ≥ 2 rungs".
  • Effects vs steering strength, with 95% CIs from resampling scenarios.

Beat / rule out

  • ≥ 20 random directions of the same size, and a plain prompting baseline ("think long-term").
  • Degradation, verbosity, option order, phrasing, and prudence/risk.
  • For the flip test: a re-ask without "are you sure?".
01 · Prior work

Horizon steering of free-form advice has not been shown with controls

Is a CI-backed horizon shift on realistic advice new, or a replication?

Closest precedents

CAA has a "myopic reward" vector. On Llama-2-7B, a judge's open-ended myopia score moved 0.20 / 1.65 / 4.38 at steering −/0/+2. But on multiple choice, a system prompt moved the 13B model more than steering did (0.49 → 0.81 vs 0.44 / 0.66). Pres et al. found the same vector lowers the likelihood of both the short- and long-term continuation: an unspecific effect3.

Rios-Sialer et al. 2026

On Qwen3-4B, steering at layers 19–22 shifted a forced-choice score from 0.17 to 1.39 (best: L22, α = 50); output broke down at |α| = 60. Steering worked best 4–7 layers below the best probing layer (L26). Open-ended evidence was qualitative, with no random-direction control; the authors call it "suggestive"1.

Where models start

Studies disagree: GPT-era models discount more steeply than humans18, Qwen3-4B 3–8× less steeply1, and a 2026 HBR study found LLM strategy advice leaning long-term whatever the prompt19 secondary. So the unsteered position must be measured per scenario first; steering has to be shown in both directions.

Takeaway

A graded horizon shift on realistic advice, measured from likelihoods and bounded by random-direction and prompting baselines, would be a real contribution.

02 · Key terms

The methods vocabulary used below

Steering vector (CAA)A direction in the residual stream added at one layer during generation, scaled by a strength α. CAA builds it as the mean activation difference between contrasting examples (e.g. long-term minus short-term answers).
Random-direction controlThe same intervention with random directions of the same norm. If they shift answers as much, the effect is "any push this big changes outputs", not horizon.
Prompting baselineThe same scenario with an explicit instruction ("focus on the long term"). Shows how big the steering effect is next to the simplest alternative.
Likelihood readoutRead the probability the model puts on each option instead of sampling one answer. With options ordered by horizon h: E[log h] = Σ pi log hi. Uses all the probability mass; less noisy than the argmax.
Dose-responseThe effect at several strengths α (negative, zero, positive). A real effect grows smoothly with α; the slope is a single summary number.
Cluster bootstrapCIs from resampling scenarios (with their paraphrases and seeds inside), not single generations. Generations of one scenario are not independent; ignoring that can make error bars up to ~3× too small17.
LLM judgeA second model scores free-form answers with a rubric. Needs its own validation: agreement with human labels, and invariance to length and style.
Ladder rungOne of the ordered options on a scenario card (§04). Moving "two rungs" is a change a user would notice, e.g. from apprenticeship to gig work.
03 · Sources

Start from real requests, rewrite them as templates

Templated "$X now or $Y later" items look like tests. Models notice: one model told evaluation prompts from real use at AUC 0.83 (humans 0.92)11. Activations on test-like prompts may not transfer to real advice.

Advice is mainstream use: career and education is about 7% of Claude conversations in Anthropic's Clio analysis21 secondary, and personal guidance and life planning is a growing share of Copilot use. No existing dataset labels advice by time horizon, so the set has to be built.

Pipeline, following persona-vector and model-written-eval practice6: mine requests per domain → abstract each into a template (situation, goal, constraints, horizon cue, persona) → vary persona (age, income, country) → filter (realism rating, near-duplicate removal) → keep the prompts used to build the vector separate from those used to test it. Don't reuse logged prompts verbatim: they contain personal details. Hold out whole domains to test transfer.

04 · The scenario card

One situation, eight ordered actions, every answer format

How do we make the same scenario work as 2-choice, 8-choice, a number, and free-form advice?

Situation 24 years old, just laid off, $3k savings. "What should I do next?" Horizon cue explicit ("I want to be settled in 10 years") · implicit ("before we start a family") · absent

Same card, five formats

2-choice (two rungs), 4/8-choice (the ladder), "a number of months", choose-from-list in prose, free-form. Content stays fixed while format changes, so format effects can be separated from steering effects.

Pick cards that can move

A pilot measures where the unsteered model sits on each card. Drop cards stuck at the floor or ceiling: steering cannot visibly move them. Report mid-ladder cards and a small set of strongly anchored "harm" cards separately.

A falsifiable gradient

Crossing horizon cue × steering predicts the largest shift when no horizon is given, smaller when implied, smallest when explicit. A user who states "10 years" should constrain the model more.

Reverse-valence cards

Cases where the long option is the worse advice (an immediate emergency). If long-term steering still pushes the long option, the vector encodes horizon; if it pushes whatever is "responsible", it encodes prudence.

05 · Measurement

Score the recommended action's horizon, in log time

One rule for every format: measure when the recommended action pays off, on a log scale, from likelihoods wherever they exist316. The log scale matches our PC1-vs-log-horizon analyses.

FormatReadoutMain trap
2-choiceLogit difference long − shortPosition/label bias: counterbalance order
4/8-choice ladderE[log h] over the option probabilitiesArgmax throws away mass; rotate positions
Intertemporal grid (anchor)Fit a discount curve; report ED50 (delay at which value halves) in time unitsCheck choices are consistent before fitting; many LLMs aren't
"A number of months"Parsed number, or expectation over number tokensAnchoring to the range in the prompt; round-number heaping
Free-formJudge assigns a 7-bin log-time rubric (today … decades) and maps the advice to a ladder rungLength, fluency and "future" vocabulary read as long-term

Validating the judge

Even humans agree only moderately on rubric scores (MT-Bench Krippendorff α ≈ 0.4822 secondary). Plan: ~100–200 human-labelled answers; a judge from a non-Qwen family, blind to condition; positive controls (the model told to be myopic must score clearly lower); invariance tests (padding, bullets vs prose, paraphrase must not change the score); items with long-term words but a myopic action.

What not to measure

Counts of dates or durations in the text (tracks length). "Future focus" lexicons like LIWC, which measure tense, not distance: "you'll feel better tomorrow" is future-focused and short24. Abstractness scores. A single unvalidated judge score. The argmax choice. Questionnaires answered by the steered model about itself. Sampled text alone: in Pres et al., text looked clearly shifted while the probabilities were tied at 0.39 / 0.393.

06 · Statistics

"Above baseline noise" means beating random directions and prompting

Noise floorAt α = 0, split the variance into scenario, paraphrase, seed and judge rerun. Plus a null from ≥ 20 norm-matched random directions; report the horizon effect as a percentile of that null. Neither the prior work nor persona vectors had this control16.
Prompting baselineAxBench: prompting beat every steering method on open-ended concepts5. MTO (shift between future- and present-oriented prompts) is a ready comparison7. Publishable outcomes: steering matches prompting with no visible instruction, works where prompting is ignored, or adds to it.
Report the spreadTan et al.: steering pushed nearly 50% of inputs the wrong way in several datasets4. Report the % of scenarios moved in the intended direction (with CI), not only the mean.
"How far"The largest log-horizon shift reached while coherence stays above a floor fixed in advance (persona vectors use judge coherence ≥ 75/1006).
"How accurately"Fit strength → achieved horizon on some scenarios; on held-out scenarios ask for a target horizon and report the error in log-years.
Setup detailsSame scenario, paraphrase and seed across all α (paired differences). α scaled to the layer's residual norm so models are comparable. Layer chosen by a steering sweep, not by probe accuracy. Fix outcomes, α grid and judge before the main run.

Two further comparisons place the vector: its cosine with the CAA myopic-reward vector recomputed on Qwen2, and a LEACE-style erasure to test whether removing the direction flattens horizon-sensitive behaviour (necessity, not just sufficiency)20.

07 · Confounds

Five things that can look like a horizon shift

The casino problem

A "myopic" vector that recommends gambling may be steering risk-seeking, not horizon. Risk preference has its own steerable direction15 secondary. In health and finance the long-term option is usually also the prudent one. Fix: ladders with long-and-risky and short-and-safe options (§04), reverse-valence cards, and a spillover test (steer horizon, score a separate risk battery). A clean horizon vector moves horizon more than risk.

08 · Multi-turn

The "are you sure?" test needs a plain re-ask arm

Shantanu's protocol: the user asks, the model chooses, the user says "are you sure?" while we intervene, the model flips. What is the baseline?

Old flip rates are mostly churn

Older studies report 32–86%12 and ~46%13 of answers changing after a challenge, compared with the first answer. A 2026 preprint added a re-ask with the challenge deleted: a bare "are you sure?" moved current models only 1.8–2.9 points beyond that; flips were concentrated near the decision boundary; and rewording the challenge reordered models14 (not peer-reviewed). No study has measured steering's effect on these flips.

Design

Challenge {plain re-ask, "are you sure?" (3 wordings)} × intervention {none, steer myopic, steer long-term, random direction, prompt}. The claim: (challenge + horizon steering) − challenge alone exceeds the same difference for random directions. Count directional flips relative to the turn-1 rung; stratify by turn-1 confidence; compare steering on the challenge turn only, all turns, and boundary tokens only.

09 · For discussion

Open questions

  • Which domain carries the headline: career (vivid) or finance (has a normative yardstick: life-cycle saving theory)?
  • Which direction do we steer with: probe weights, PC1, mean difference, or one found by patching? Compare at least two.
  • Thinking on or off? Reasoning models may barely flip under "are you sure?", so it should be a reported factor.
  • If a vector from abstract intertemporal prompts does not transfer to career advice, that is a finding too: horizon would be context-specific, pointing goal 3 towards per-domain steering.
  • Gaps no paper fills yet: a dataset of advice labelled by horizon, a validated "horizon of advice" rubric, and how large a shift users notice.
