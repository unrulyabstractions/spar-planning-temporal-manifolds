## Reading

1. Horizon is a small share of activation variance at these cells (1–13%); rendering × domain offsets
   carry 60–85%. With offsets, a shared curve predicts held-out scenarios and withheld horizons at the
   training fit; interaction terms add ≤ 0.035. → the horizon curve is shared across presentation and
   domain up to constant shifts (at L26 T1 the interaction gain is largest, 0.03).
2. Line, quadratic and spline are indistinguishable in forward R² because the horizon share is small;
   the centroid error (0.35–0.6 of within-bin spread at withheld horizons) is the discriminating
   forward metric, and it favours interaction/spline slightly.
3. Basis-free shape (quad): the 1 d–100 y curve turns ~120–130° end to end with tortuosity ~1.4,
   curvature peaking at 0.1–0.4 y; the spline puts 95% of the curve's variance in 3–4 dimensions
   (top-3 shares ≈ 0.74/0.16/0.05), i.e. mildly non-planar, consistent with the extended-grid PC3.
   Bootstrap intervals are tight; the shape is not a sampling accident.
4. Absolute horizon beats relative-to-option-delay at every cell (by 0.005–0.03 R²): the curve is
   parameterized by absolute duration.
5. The curve decoder does not earn its complexity: 0.5–0.7 within-2× vs ridge 0.9 (projection onto a
   1-D curve that carries ~1% of variance is noisy). Ridge stays the primary decoder.
6. Distractors act off-curve: the role distractor ("you have 2 hours") moves activations orthogonally
   to the curve by 3× the baseline residual, in proportion to |D−H| (rho +0.67–0.69), with little
   systematic along-curve shift; the mention distractor raises the orthogonal residual less and
   uncorrelated with |D−H|. So "durations mentioned" is a separate direction, not a magnitude misread;
   projecting onto the curve removes most of the mention pull (ridge +0.58 → curve +0.13 at L22 T3) but
   not the role pull, and at a large accuracy cost. Extrapolation to the extended-grid extremes fails
   for every model (negative R²): the curve is not the same object outside 1 d–100 y.
