## Metric correction (affects how the step-3 pull numbers should be read)

The step-3 "pull" was rho(prediction − H, D − H). A ridge prediction is attenuated toward the mean, so
its residual correlates with −H and hence with D − H even with no distractor effect (D is drawn
independently of H). Verified in the planted test: naive residual correlation 0.13 after the nuisance is
fully removed vs pair-based pull −0.006. New definitions (both from the 240 matched pairs per control
type; the distractor-free partner cancels attenuation):
- pull  = Spearman(Δpred, log D − log H), Δpred = prediction(control) − prediction(partner)
- slope = least-squares slope of Δpred on (log D − log H), in decades per decade (1 = follows the distractor)
Protocol: nuisance subspace N from 6 of the 10 control configurations; pull/slope measured on the other 4;
ridge trained on train configs × 3 renderings × 8 training horizons; accuracy = within-2x on new scenarios.

## Reading against the kill criteria

1. Mention ("established D ago"): the distractor's effect is mostly a CONSTANT displacement, not a
   magnitude leak — the slope is ≤ 0.08 decades/decade even for plain ridge, while accuracy on mention
   rows is poor (0.43–0.44) at two cells because of a fixed offset. Projecting out ≤ 3 mention
   directions removes it (mention w2x 0.43 → 0.82, 0.44 → 0.80; pull → ≤ 0.16) at no cost on new
   scenarios. Hypothesis survives for this type.
2. Role ("you have D to prepare"): a genuine magnitude leak (slope 0.24–0.29, pull 0.75–0.94). Projection
   needs k = 5 to bring the slope to 0.05–0.09 and leaves role-row accuracy at 0.52–0.75; the effect is
   not compactly separable at these cells. Cross-type transfer fails in both directions.
3. Training with control prompts (the data route) is the best decoder: slopes 0.00–0.05 for both types,
   control-row accuracy 0.76–0.95, new-scenario accuracy 0.91–0.93. Residual rank pull stays (0.33–0.60
   for role) but its magnitude is a few hundredths of a decade.
4. The first nuisance direction is orthogonal to the ridge weight (88–90°): the pull is not carried by the
   dominant displacement direction but by its tails; and it is 76–86° off the horizon-curve plane
   (off-manifold, as step 4 found).
5. Caveat: "you have 2 hours to prepare" is itself a temporal instruction about the response, so part of
   the role effect may be a legitimate representation, not noise; the behavioral test (choices ignore it)
   is the reason to call it a distractor for the decision.

## Reading

1. The data route generalizes: adding distractor families to training reduces the slope and raises
   accuracy on a never-seen family roughly linearly (~5 points per family at L22 T3), reaching the
   own-family ceiling at two of three cells with five families. Projection of a fixed nuisance subspace
   does not generalize at any rank.
2. The ceiling itself is 0.68–0.78 within-2x (vs 0.91–0.93 on distractor-free prompts): even a known
   family costs 15–25 points of accuracy at these positions.
3. Role ("you have D to prepare") stays hard at m=5 (slope 0.22): it is the most distinct family
   geometrically (53–67° from the others) and behaves less like an irrelevant mention.
4. Family subspaces are partially shared (35–49°) yet projecting the shared part does nothing, so the
   pull rides on components outside each family's top directions; augmentation, which reweights the
   whole readout, is the mechanism that works.
→ A relevance-robust linear monitor at boundary tokens is plausible with a broad, diverse distractor
training set; the number of families needed exceeds five, and the residual cost is ~15–25 points.
