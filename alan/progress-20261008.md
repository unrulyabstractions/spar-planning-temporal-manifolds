  (Correction to what I told Alan from the partial data: "option order barely matters" was wrong — I read P(a) as order
  sensitivity.)
- Reasoning audit (sampled gen_text): below-boundary violations are real comparison errors ("Over the 29.64-quarter horizon,
  the $50,000 received in 10 years (40 quarters) is still within the evaluation period"), sometimes contradicting the stated
  reasoning ("5.72 quarters (approximately 1.43 years) is shorter than the 2-year maturity of option b" → chose b).
  Above-boundary "errors" often are not errors: correct conversion then a liquidity preference ("completes its investment
  cycle in 1 year, which is well within the 2.61-year planning horizon"). So below-boundary violation rates are the clean
  metric; above-boundary short choices partly reflect preference.

Reading: Qwen3.8-27B applies the horizon constraint well when horizon and delays share a unit — in all four units and in
both orders, far better than Qwen3-14B. When they differ, the boundary disappears for any pair involving weeks or quarters
(the choice no longer depends on H/D) and shifts only ≈ 5% for months ↔ years. The 14B finding was the months/years corner
of a much larger effect, not an artefact of a weak model.
