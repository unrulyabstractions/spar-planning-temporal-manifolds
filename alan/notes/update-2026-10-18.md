## Extra slide 2 (closing) — what would tell the three failures apart?

Without thinking, the model gets mismatched-unit prompts wrong. Three candidate explanations, and the result that would
pick each out:

| failure | the model… | behavioural signature | readout signature (pre-answer, on error prompts) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conversion** | mis-scales one quantity | switch point shifted by a fixed factor per unit pair, opposite for the reverse pair; fixed by giving the conversion in the prompt | decoded log D (or log H) off for the mismatched unit |
| **Comparison** | has both durations, compares them badly | curve still depends on H/D but flatter (errors on both sides of the boundary); fixed by stating which option falls inside the horizon, not by the conversion alone | log H and log D right, sign(H − D) wrong or at chance |
| **Constraint use** | knows the answer, doesn't act on it | P(long) flat in H/D, driven by option order and reward; stating the comparison doesn't fix it | sign(H − D) right, choice ignores it |

- **Existing evidence:** both months↔years directions shift toward long → not a single mis-scaling; many
  weeks/quarters cells are flat in H/D and order-dominated → looks like constraint use, untested.
- **Cheap behavioural arms (no activations):** (a) the conversion given in the prompt ("40 quarters (10 years)");
  (b) the comparison given ("option b) pays out after your horizon"). Fixed by (a) → conversion; by (b) only →
  comparison; by neither → constraint use.
- **Causal version, with Jared's steering** (along the curve, which moved Qwen3-8B toward the rational answer by 0.39
  of the contrast at thinking-off pre-label L24): steer the horizon on mismatched-unit prompts. Conversion or comparison
  failure → the switch point moves as it does with matched units; constraint-use failure → the flat cells stay flat.
- **Then the readout** (C2 of 10-13) on a newer model, probes trained on matched units, tested on mismatched errors.
