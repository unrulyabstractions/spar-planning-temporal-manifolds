# Progress since the 2026-09-25 snapshot — plain-language summary (Alan, 2026-10-01)

The 09-25 snapshot held the Qwen3-14B replication, the matched prompt matrix, the frozen-decoder transfer
results, and the first relevance controls. Everything below was added between 09-26 and 09-30. Every number
quoted here is in the dated logs (`progress-202609{27,28,29,30}.md`) together with the command that produced
it, and all of it regenerates from committed code. Model names: "14B" = Qwen/Qwen3-14B (40 layers), "27B" =
Qwen/Qwen3.5-27B (64 layers, hybrid linear attention), "9B" = Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B (32 layers, local pilot).

## 1. What the horizon representation looks like: a curve, a ladder, and a correction

**A forward model of the curve (step 4).** Instead of asking "can we read the horizon out", we fitted the
reverse map: activation = baseline + f(log horizon) + offset(prompt style), where prompt style is one of
six rendering × domain combinations (structured / plain / varied prose × investment / climate). Most of
the activation variance is style, not horizon: the horizon curve alone explains 1–13% of it at the cells
tested (0.014 held-out R² at L22 T3), and adding the constant per-style offsets brings the fit to 0.83–0.85
held-out at L22 T3 and L22 R0 (0.63 at L26 T1). The finding is the structure, not that total: one horizon
curve is shared by all six styles up to a constant shift (letting the curve differ per style gains ≤ 0.003
R² at L22 T3 and L22 R0, 0.035 at L26 T1), and the fit holds on new scenarios, on withheld horizons, and on
both at once at the training level. Line, quadratic and spline are within 0.005 R² of each other, because
horizon carries so little of the variance. The curve is flat and smooth: tortuosity ≈ 1.4, end-to-end turn ≈ 120°. Inverting the curve as a
decoder is worse than ridge (0.5–0.7 vs 0.9 within a factor of two), so the forward model is for describing
geometry, not for reading horizons out.

**The "bend at one year" is withdrawn.** Shantanu pointed out that an arch in PC1–PC2 is the generic
signature of ordinal data whose similarity decays with distance (the Guttman or horseshoe effect). We tested
that directly. Refitting the parabola on sliding 9-bin windows, its vertex moves with the window midpoint
with slope ≈ 1 (0.98–1.16 on the 14B; 0.82–1.07 on the 27B), exactly as a banded-similarity null predicts.
The full-range vertex sits at the middle of the log grid. So "the curve turns over near one year" says
nothing about the model; it says where the middle of our horizon range is. What survives the null is
more interesting: the real centroids are *more* concentrated in one direction than a banded process (PC1
share 0.58–0.85 vs 0.36–0.48 for matched nulls) and PC3 is not the cubic the null predicts. Centroid
distances are a function of |Δ log horizon| with a decay length of ≈ 1.7–2.4 decades.

**How many dimensions, measured properly.** We added the number of components for 90% / 95% of variance
to every PCA report, and then a supervised, rotation-free version of the polynomial ladder (Shantanu's
PLS2 suggestion, implemented and found to break down under the residual stream's nuisance variance;
replaced by reduced-rank ridge regression with PLS2 kept as the comparison). Result: every polynomial
degree of log horizon up to 4 is decodable on held-out scenarios — so "number of decodable dimensions" is
not a meaningful count with a finite set of horizon levels — but the *variance* carried by the degrees
falls geometrically: about 0.6 / 0.25 / 0.03 / 0.02 on 1 day–100 years, with the higher terms growing as the
range widens (0.4 / 0.35 / 0.10 / 0.02 on 1 minute–10,000 years). "Two-dimensional" is a 90%-of-variance
statement, with a real but small tail. Same on the 27B.

**Why pooled PCA never finds horizon on mixed prompts.** We erased the nuisance factors (rendering, domain,
scenario parameters, prompt identity) with LEACE, fitted on training scenarios and applied to unseen ones.
The erasure is exact (rendering and domain probes drop to chance; horizon decodability is untouched). Yet
pooled PCA recovers horizon on PC1 only at some depths and positions: on the 14B at the first response
token from layer 26 on (|ρ| 0.85–0.88), not at the transition tokens. The reason is not just variance
budget: the horizon *direction* differs by 55–63° between prompt renderings at the transition tokens and by
only 28° where erasure works. There are two depths: an early one where each prompt style has its own
horizon axis, and a later one where the axis is shared. The 27B has the same two stages (61–63° at its
early peak cells, 36–39° at 0.55–0.72 of depth).

**Stakes.** The absolute reward size (1,000–100,000) is its own ordinal axis in the activations, as reliable
as horizon's and orthogonal to it (80–88°), with a per-decade response comparable to horizon's at mid
depth. It does not bend or move the horizon curve (directions within 6–15°, chords equal, decoders transfer
across stakes strata at 0.90–0.99). Behaviourally the 14B ignores it; the 27B chooses the short option more
often at higher stakes (standardised coefficient +0.30 [+0.15, +0.45]). Same geometry, different use.

## 2. The relevance problem: what moves the decoder vs what moves the model

**Metric correction.** The 09-25 "pull" numbers were inflated by regression to the mean. The replacement
metrics use matched pairs (prompt with an irrelevant duration minus the same prompt without it): a rank
correlation of the within-pair change with the distractor offset, and a slope in decades per decade.

**Mention vs role.** An irrelevant mentioned duration ("established 20 years ago") is a constant
displacement: the slope is ≤ 0.08 even for plain ridge, and projecting out ≤ 3 directions estimated from
other scenarios restores accuracy on those prompts (0.43 → 0.82). A duration in the model's own role ("you
have 2 hours to prepare") is a genuine magnitude leak (slope 0.24–0.29) that projection removes only at a
large accuracy cost. Training the decoder on prompts that contain distractors (the data route) is the best
decoder: slopes 0.00–0.05 for both, 0.76–0.95 accuracy on distractor prompts, no loss on clean ones. This
converges with Augusto's independent relevance-aware decoder (same paired metric, same conclusion).

**Does it generalize to distractor kinds never seen?** Four new families (option age, future event,
frequency, elapsed planning time). Every unseen family displaces a decoder trained without it. A
leave-one-family-out test shows the data route generalizes linearly: each added family buys ≈ 5 points
of accuracy on an unseen one, reaching the own-family ceiling (0.68–0.78) with five; fixed projections do
not generalize. Role stays hard.

**A late readout that is robust at zero cost.** We added anchored positions: the first reasoning token,
the last generated token, and the mean over the model's reasoning. The reasoning-mean readout is
relevance-robust with no augmentation at all: slope 0.02–0.04, 0.84 accuracy on distractor prompts, 0.90 on
new scenarios (14B, layer 26); the 27B reproduces it (slope 0.03–0.04, 0.80–0.89). The transition-token
readout at the same layers has slope ≈ 0.2. Where the model has already begun to reason, the
representation it carries is the one that respects relevance.

## 3. The 27B run

The whole chain (5 datasets, 8 captures incl. the distractor families and the long anchored captures, every
analysis above, subset export, checksums) was made model-agnostic — layer specs by fractional depth, a
single runbook — verified bit-exact on a hybrid linear-attention model (hooks vs library hidden states at
all 64 layers), and run on a rented A100 80 GB (≈ 14 h, ≈ $20; a clean rerun would be ≈ 5 h, ≈ $7 — most of
the time was lost to a container CPU quota that starved the capture, now handled). 37 GB of tier-1
activation subsets (13 layers × all positions × 8 runs) were copied back and verified by checksum; the
prompt datasets generated on the instance are byte-identical to the local ones.

What the 27B shows:
- Everything replicates: ordinality 0.97 with the reward control ≤ 0.04; the planar arc (0.6 / 0.25 / 0.04);
  the Guttman diagnosis; the ladder; the two-depth story; mixed-rendering transfer (0.83–1.00 within 2× on
  suites A–G, structured-only training still fails on prose); distractor pull and its removal; the robust
  reasoning-mean readout; the stakes axis.
- Depth differs: the single-scenario peak is at 0.3–0.5 of depth (14B: 0.55–0.92), and the first-response-
  token ordinality collapses after 0.7 of depth. The 9B pilot behaves like the 27B, so this may be a
  Qwen3 → Qwen3.5 generation difference rather than scale. The fixed-fraction cells from the prior work are
  not the right coordinate for cross-model comparison; a script now picks cells from the sweep.
- Behaviour is cleaner and one new effect appears: P(short) 1.00 up to 1 y, 0.5 at 10 y, 0.24 at 30–50 y;
  and the stakes effect above.
