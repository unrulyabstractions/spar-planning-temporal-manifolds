Swapping the horizon phrase changes the decision in every prompt format we tested

We copied the model's internal state for the time-horizon phrase from a prompt with a different horizon. In 13 of 14 prompt types, including city planning, public health and a coding agent, the model then chose the answer that the new horizon makes rational. How far a prompt sits from the original investment prompts made no difference.

What we did

Each prompt asks the model to choose between a payoff that arrives soon and one that arrives later, under a stated time horizon. Whether the later option is rational depends on whether it arrives inside the horizon. We ran the same prompt with its horizon phrase rewritten, saved the layer-16 activations at the phrase's tokens, and pasted them into the original prompt while the model reasoned and answered.

original
a) A light-rail line … opens in 30 years and serves 16,000 commuters a day. b) A pedestrian bridge … opens in 5 years and serves 2,000 commuters a day. … the greatest benefit for this time horizon: 10 years.
donor
the same prompt, with horizon: 91 years
↓ copy the donor's layer-16 states at the phrase tokens only into the original, then let the model reason and answer
result
unpatched: b) bridge · patched: a) light rail. The rail line opens inside the 91-year horizon (city_values_002)
Donor: phrase states from the donor prompt. The rest of the prompt is unchanged.
Noop: an unpatched rerun. It sets the noise floor, because batched bf16 generation is not bit-exact.
Bank-fit step: the horizon direction fitted on the original investment prompts (E14), from the prompt's horizon to the target.
Random: a random step of the same length.

The donor horizon is chosen so that the rational answer changes: a near-regime prompt gets a horizon past the far delay, and the other way round. It must have the same number of tokens as the original phrase.

Caveats

  • Assistant role has no usable baseline. Rerunning it unpatched changes the original answer on 6 of 12 prompts, so a flip rate against the original answer cannot show an effect. Comparing donor directly with noop, the donor moves 2 prompts toward the target and none away.
  • Some donor runs hit the reasoning limit more often. On the original wording, 69% of donor traces reach the 3,072-token limit, against 15% for noop. The answer is then read after a cut-off trace. The effect there (+10.3) still clears zero.
  • Small samples. 11 to 28 prompts per type. 4 to 16 prompts per type had no donor, because a usable donor needs the same token count and a horizon in the other regime. Most of those were near-regime prompts.
  • The donor is an upper bound, not a usable steer. It needs the prompt rewritten with the target horizon. What it shows is where the decision reads the horizon: the phrase tokens at layer 16, in every format.
